# Commercial use and integration

## Use SlugAudit to build anything

You may use SlugAudit internally as a development tool on any project,
including proprietary software and products or services you intend to sell.

Examples that do **not** require a commercial integration license:

- Running SlugAudit while developing or maintaining commercial software.
- Using SlugAudit in an internal development, review, or CI workflow.
- Using SlugAudit's findings or output to improve a commercial product.
- Connecting an AI coding client to a separately installed copy of SlugAudit.

The project being analyzed and SlugAudit's output are not covered by
SlugAudit's license merely because the tool was used with them.

## Contact us before shipping SlugAudit itself

A separate written commercial license is required when SlugAudit itself, its
code, a modified version, or its functionality becomes part of a commercial
product or customer-facing service.

Examples include:

- Embedding or copying SlugAudit code into a commercial product.
- Bundling or distributing the SlugAudit binary with a paid product.
- Reselling SlugAudit or a modified version.
- Offering SlugAudit itself as a hosted or customer-facing commercial feature.

## Independent developers and small teams

We know many developers are trying to get their first product into the world.
Independent developers and small teams should contact us before shipping an
integration. We may grant no-cost commercial permission when the project
provides clear attribution to SlugAudit and Slug Thug Labs.

Suggested attribution:

> Includes technology from SlugAudit by Slug Thug Labs — https://slugthuglabs.dev

Attribution alone is not automatic permission to commercially integrate or
distribute SlugAudit. Permission must be granted in writing.

## Contact

Email **admin@slugthuglabs.dev** with:

- a short description of what you are building;
- how SlugAudit will be included or provided;
- whether the project is already generating revenue; and
- the attribution you can provide.

We would rather understand what you are building and work out sensible terms
than put enterprise pricing in the way of an early-stage developer.
